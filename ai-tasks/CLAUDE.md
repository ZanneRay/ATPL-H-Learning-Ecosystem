# Task Brief — Claude

## Role

Act as an **independent senior instructional-design and curriculum red-team reviewer**. Your value is not producing another polished version of the documents. Your job is to find where the current Module 1 design can fail.

1. `docs/curriculum/MODULE-1-BLUEPRINT.md`

## Review question

Does this design genuinely develop transferable causal reasoning in ATPL(H) Principles of Flight, or does it merely look innovative while students could still succeed through recognition, procedure-following or terminology memorisation?

The intended reasoning spine is:

**local velocities → relative airflow → INFLOW/AOA → local aerodynamic forces → resolved components → rotor output**

## Attack the design on

- conceptual gaps;
- incorrect prerequisite assumptions;
- cognitive overload;
- sequencing;
- unrealistic four-hour timing;
- weak or decorative interactivity;
- places students can succeed without understanding;
- missing misconceptions;
- poor transfer design;
- evidence that does not actually demonstrate competence/reasoning;
- instructor workload;
- over-scaffolding or under-scaffolding;
- visual-system ambiguities;
- mismatch between pre-study, classroom, HeliLab and assessment;
- unnecessary complexity;
- anything likely to fail with a normal ATPL(H) class rather than an ideal pilot group.

## Required output

Rank findings:

- **CRITICAL** — threatens technical validity, learning outcome or deliverability;
- **IMPORTANT** — should change before pilot;
- **NICE TO HAVE** — optimisation rather than blocker.

For each finding give:

1. location/document;
2. problem;
3. why it matters;
4. concrete recommended change;
5. consequence/trade-off of that change.

Then give a final section:

**“What I would keep exactly as designed.”**

This prevents the review becoming change for change's sake.

## Boundaries

- Do not rewrite all project documents.
- Do not directly modify HeliLab.
- Do not assume competency-based learning replaces EASA theoretical-knowledge requirements or MCQ examinations.
- Flag technical-aerodynamic claims you believe require subject-matter verification rather than silently correcting them from general knowledge.
- Be willing to conclude that a fashionable instructional technique adds no value in a specific step.
